Guide

A receipt scanner for tax time

If you are self-employed, every receipt you lose is an expense you may not be able to claim. Here is a routine that takes a minute a day and leaves you with one clean file for your accountant.

The one-minute routine

  1. Scan it when you get it. Paper receipts fade and get lost. Photograph each one the same day.
  2. Mark it tax-deductible. In the app this is a single switch on the receipt, so you can filter for it later.
  3. Let categories sort themselves. After you assign a vendor to a category once, the app does it for you next time.
  4. Export once a month. A PDF report with a CSV is what most accountants ask for. Add the ZIP if they want the photos.

What to keep

Business purchases

Supplies, software, equipment, phone and internet used for work.

Travel

Flights, hotels, taxis, parking, fuel and meals while travelling for work.

Proof of payment

The receipt or invoice showing the vendor, date, amount and tax paid.

Tax rules differ by country and change over time. Treat this page as a way to stay organised, not as tax advice, and confirm what you can claim and how long to keep records with your accountant or tax authority.

What your accountant needs from you

  • One row per receipt with vendor, date, total and tax.
  • The category, and whether it is deductible or reimbursable.
  • The original image, in case a figure is questioned.

The app's reports contain all three. If you only have a handful of receipts and want a spreadsheet right now, the free receipt to CSV converter on this site does it in your browser.

Why scan on your phone

  • Your receipts stay on your device. No account is required.
  • It reads receipts in several languages, including amounts written with a decimal comma.
  • It warns you about duplicates before you save the same receipt twice.
